The Tokenization Surge Moves From Pilot to Product

Tokenized equities and treasuries stopped being demonstrations this year. The reporting problem now is measuring them consistently.

Market Watch

From demonstration to distribution

For several years tokenization existed mostly as proof of concept: a fund wrapper here, a pilot bond issue there, each announced with more enthusiasm than volume. That period has ended. Tokenized treasuries and equity-style instruments now clear meaningful daily flow, and consumer platforms have started issuing them directly to retail users rather than to institutional counterparties.

The shift creates a measurement problem. Different venues count tokenized value differently — some report gross issuance, others net of redemptions, and a few include wrapped positions that are also counted on the underlying chain. Comparing headline numbers across issuers without normalising them produces nonsense.

Our approach is to publish the issuer-level figures and the definition used for each, rather than a single aggregate that hides the methodology.

  • Tokenized treasuries and equities now clear real daily volume.
  • Issuers use inconsistent definitions of tokenized value.
  • Retail distribution changes the redemption profile.

The risks inside the growth

Tokenization does not remove the underlying asset's risk; it relocates the settlement layer. A tokenized equity still carries market risk, and it adds smart-contract risk, issuer solvency risk and the operational risk of whoever maintains the redemption path.

Redemption is the pressure point. During calm markets the wrapper is invisible; during a fast drawdown the question of who honours redemptions, on what timetable, and under whose jurisdiction becomes the entire story.

Regulatory treatment also remains fragmented. The same instrument can be a security, a derivative or a payment token depending on the venue, and issuers have been noticeably quiet about which regime governs the products they distribute.
