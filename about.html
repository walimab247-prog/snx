Our story

Who we are

CryptoNewsLantern was founded in 2021 by a small group of reporters, on-chain analysts and former exchange engineers who were tired of watching important stories arrive twelve hours late and wrapped in marketing language. We started as a daily briefing for a few hundred professional readers and grew into a full newsroom covering markets, protocols, regulation and security.

Today the publication reaches more than 640,000 monthly readers across 130 countries, publishes in a single editorial voice, and keeps a strict wall between editorial and commercial work. No article is ever sold, and no coverage decision is ever made by a sponsor.

Our team is distributed across Lisbon, Singapore and Toronto, which lets us keep a newsroom awake through every major trading session. Breaking market events do not wait for office hours, and neither do we.

How we work

Every market claim we publish is tied to a source a reader can inspect: an on-chain transaction, a governance record, an exchange filing or a named person speaking on the record. Where a number cannot be verified we say so in the sentence, not in a footnote.

Price and supply data is pulled from multiple independent providers and cross-checked before publication. When two sources disagree by more than a small tolerance, the story states the range rather than picking the more dramatic figure.

Corrections are permanent and visible. If we get something wrong, the article carries a dated correction note explaining exactly what changed and why, and the correction is pushed to the same channels that carried the original story.

Standards

Editorial principles

Independence is the product. We do not accept payment for coverage, we do not publish sponsored articles disguised as reporting, and members of the editorial team disclose any digital asset holdings that could create a conflict of interest.

We avoid price predictions. Instead we explain mechanisms: why a stablecoin lost its peg, how a bridge exploit was executed, what a governance vote actually changes in the contract layer. Readers can form their own view once they understand the machinery.

Nothing we publish is investment advice. Our job is to describe what happened, show the evidence and outline the plausible consequences.

  • No paid coverage, ever — commercial partnerships are labelled and kept off the news pages.
  • Named sourcing wherever possible; anonymous sourcing only when there is a demonstrable risk to the speaker.
  • Every security incident report links to the on-chain evidence used to build it.
  • Staff disclose holdings and step away from stories where a conflict exists.
