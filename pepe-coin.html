Synthetix Community Votes to Decommission Depegged sUSD, Offers SNX Compensation

The Synthetix governance process has approved the retirement of sUSD after a depeg that dragged the token to roughly a quarter of its dollar target. Holders are offered SNX instead — but on a long lock-up.

Read the market impactProtocol status board

Governance

The vote that ends a legacy stablecoin

Synthetix token holders have approved a proposal to wind down sUSD, the protocol's long-running synthetic dollar, closing one of the oldest chapters in decentralised finance. The measure, put forward by Synthetix founder Kain Warwick together with core contributor Benjamin Celermajer, retires the token on both Ethereum mainnet and Optimism rather than attempting yet another peg rescue.

The decision follows more than a year of failed stabilisation attempts. After the protocol migrated debt into a protocol-owned pool, individual stakers no longer had a direct economic reason to defend the peg. sUSD slid below seventy cents within weeks and eventually settled near the twenty-five cent area, where liquidity thinned out and market makers stepped away from the pair.

Instead of allowing the token to fade into an illiquid long tail, governance chose a structured decommissioning: freeze the supply, snapshot ownership, and convert every remaining unit into the protocol's native SNX token at a fixed, above-market rate.

  • sUSD is retired on Ethereum mainnet and Optimism.
  • Minting, burning and transfers stop after the governance snapshot.
  • The legacy debt pool is closed and the sUSD staking ratio requirement is removed.

Key numbers

The headline parameters approved by Synthetix governance for the sUSD wind-down.

4 SNX
Per 1 sUSD

Every unit of sUSD converts into four SNX, valuing sUSD at face value against an assumed SNX price of twenty-five cents.

1 + 1 yrs
Lock and vesting

Compensation is locked for a full year, then vests linearly across a second year rather than unlocking at once.

6 months
Claim window

Holders must file a claim inside the six-month window after it opens or their entitlement can expire.

How the compensation actually works

A snapshot is taken at a governance-defined block so that the register of holders cannot be gamed after the announcement. Balances sitting in liquidity pools, vaults and third-party contracts are recognised, but many of them require a separate claiming flow rather than an automatic credit.

The conversion ratio is deliberately generous relative to the market: at the time of the vote sUSD traded far below the dollar, so a fixed four-to-one exchange into SNX hands holders a paper premium. That premium is not free. The tokens are illiquid for twelve months and then release gradually, which transfers the risk from a broken peg into exposure to the SNX price and to time.

Participants who still carry debt against the protocol have two routes: repay in full for an early exit, or accept a four-year lock followed by the vesting schedule. A supplementary proposal handles the technical migration steps, contract freezes and claim contracts.

Analysis

Why the peg failed in the first place

sUSD was never a reserve-backed stablecoin. It was minted against staked SNX, which means its stability depended on stakers being paid enough to keep collateral in the system and to arbitrage the peg back to a dollar. When the incentive structure moved debt into a pooled, protocol-managed model, that arbitrage loop weakened.

The result was a slow-motion depeg rather than a sudden collapse. Liquidity incentives, borrowing caps and repeated governance tweaks slowed the decline but never reversed it. Each failed intervention pushed more holders towards the exit and thinned the order book further, making the next defence more expensive than the last.

The contrast with algorithmic blow-ups of previous cycles matters. There was no bank-run cascade into other assets and no contagion event; instead there was a long, visible erosion that governance eventually chose to end deliberately.

Market impact

What it means for the wider market

For DeFi builders, the wind-down is a template. It shows that a protocol can retire a failed instrument through public governance, with a documented snapshot, a published conversion ratio and a defined claim period, instead of leaving users with a permanently broken asset and no recourse.

For SNX holders, the arithmetic is less comfortable. Issuing new tokens to compensate sUSD holders adds future supply, and that supply arrives on a schedule everyone can read in advance. Traders will price the unlock curve long before the first tokens move.

There is also a revenue-linked sweetener: if the protocol generates significant revenue during the lock-up period, a share of it can be distributed to legacy holders in a liquid dollar-denominated form rather than in additional SNX, subject to governance adjustment.

Synthetix itself is redirecting attention towards perpetual futures and other trading products, treating the synthetic dollar as a closed chapter rather than a product to be relaunched.

Reader questions

sUSD Wind-Down FAQ

The questions our newsroom received most often after the vote was published.

Do I need to do anything to receive SNX?

Holders recorded in the snapshot must submit a claim once the claim contract opens. Balances held inside liquidity pools, vaults or custodial products generally require an extra step, so check the specific integration you used before assuming an automatic credit.

Can I still trade or transfer sUSD?

No. After the snapshot block the token is frozen: minting, burning and transfers are disabled across the supported networks, and remaining market liquidity is expected to disappear quickly.

Is the four-to-one ratio guaranteed to be profitable?

No. The ratio values sUSD at one dollar against a fixed SNX reference price, but the tokens are locked for a year and then vest across a second year. The realised value depends entirely on where SNX trades during that release schedule.

What happens to open debt positions?

Debt holders can repay in full and exit early, or accept the extended lock and vesting arrangement approved by governance. The technical implementation is handled by a follow-up proposal.

Does this affect other Synthetix products?

The perpetual futures and derivatives side of the protocol continues to operate. The decommissioning is scoped to the legacy synthetic dollar and the debt pool that supported it.
