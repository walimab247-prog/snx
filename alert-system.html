Alert System: Threat Notifications in Minutes

How a suspicious transaction becomes a verified alert on your phone, and why we would rather be right than first.

Security

From anomaly to alert

Our alerting pipeline begins with monitoring rather than with rumours. Indexed chain data is scanned continuously for the patterns that precede or accompany an exploit: abnormal withdrawal velocity, privileged function calls from unexpected addresses, sudden imbalance in a pool that had been stable, and large approvals granted to freshly deployed contracts.

An anomaly alone is never published. It opens a case, and a human analyst checks the transaction trace, the affected contract and the destination addresses before anything reaches subscribers. The verification step usually costs a few minutes, and it is the difference between an alert readers can act on and a rumour that moves markets for the wrong reason.

Once confirmed, the alert goes out simultaneously to email subscribers and our social channels, with the exploit transaction hash included so that recipients can verify the claim themselves rather than take our word for it.

  • Continuous anomaly detection over indexed chain data.
  • Human verification before any alert is distributed.
  • Every alert includes the transaction hash behind it.

Why speed is not the only metric

Being first is easy if accuracy is optional. Automated bots routinely broadcast unconfirmed anomalies, and a meaningful share of them turn out to be routine treasury operations, migrations or test transactions. Each false alarm costs readers attention and, occasionally, money.

We therefore optimise for verified latency: the time between the exploit transaction and a confirmed, evidence-backed alert. Our target is under an hour for complex cases and a few minutes for clear-cut ones.

Alerts are also written to be actionable. Where a user action can reduce exposure — revoking an approval, withdrawing from a specific pool — the alert says so explicitly instead of leaving the reader to infer it.
